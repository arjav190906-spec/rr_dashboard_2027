    6 · Method and checks

    How the scores were built, and what was verified

    Pipeline

    • Match context. Every legal ball carries balls left, wickets down, target and required rate.
    • Two models. The supplied analysis uses a gradient-boosted outcome model and a win-probability model to price each ball’s context and leverage; scouting scores are shrunken toward league average.
    • Value over expected. A player's runs, minus a phase-priced wicket cost, minus what an average player would produce in the same situation.
    • Pressure. Top 25% of balls by swing is pressure; top 5% is critical.
    • Shrinkage. Small samples are pulled toward league average, so a hot 60-ball spell cannot top a list.
    • Need scores. Finisher: death-over value, death strike rate, pressure value, overall value. Fast bowler: overall, death, pressure, consistency. All-rounder: bowling and batting value equally, and he must face at least 7 balls per match.
    • Pick rule. Where two players sit within 3 points, the one with the larger sample wins.

    Limits to keep in mind

    • Scores screen, they do not forecast. Backtesting last season's rating against next season gave a rank correlation of only 0.17 to 0.19. Use the board to shortlist, then apply scouting judgement.
    • Player metrics contain no prices, age, injury or registration fields. The official release PDF gives old 2026 salaries, not 2027 base prices or auction values. Bid ceilings below are management guardrails, not market forecasts.
    • Evidence base is IPL and T20Is. Domestic and other leagues are absent, so young Indian players with few IPL balls score near average by design.
    • Nationality is read from the T20I team. Players without a T20I are treated as Indian and were checked by hand against the release list.
    • Role labels for the retained squad are analyst tags backed by bowling type and batting order from the data.
